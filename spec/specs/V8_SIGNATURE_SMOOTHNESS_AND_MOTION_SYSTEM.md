# V8 — THE CALM CINEMATIC MOTION SYSTEM | P0 PRODUCT CONTRACT

## The feeling (not just animation quantity)
The visitor must feel as if moving through **one continuous, tranquil architectural film**: soft, precisely directed, elegant, weighty, natural and relaxing. Motion serves wayfinding, spatial continuity and delight; it must never feel like a PowerPoint, jittery game camera, pop-up circus, flashy advertisement, or artificially slow app. It must remain immediately responsive to user input. Beauty without comfort, clarity, input responsiveness or stable frame times is FAILURE.

This document takes precedence over V1–V7 whenever motion requirements conflict. Preserve the four global sections Explore/Tour/Design/Amenities, 12 floors, 18 concept units, four Tour camera modes, four locales (AR default, EN, Sorani, ZH-CN), SOUND OFF, source-grounded Nakhla AI, all seven V7 engineering gates, safe optional Higgsfield for *offline marketing assets only*, and no unauthorized spend or deployment. **Do not add a fifth tab or a big new settings page.** A tiny contextual Motion Comfort preference is sufficient.

## Motion director / one ownership model (mandatory)
- Implement a central, typed **MotionDirector** or equivalent architecture for temporal coordination across the application. Its responsibilities: shared clock, camera pose/target/FOV, scene visibility and cutaway, UI affordances, materials, environmental transition, input lock, prefetch and cancellation/retargeting. React/router, GSAP/Motion (if chosen), Three.js and CSS must not fight over the same property.
- Camera transform has **one writer at any moment**. While a directed camera trip is running, orbit/free-look controls are temporarily blended/paused safely; on user interaction either cancel/retarget from the CURRENT rendered pose and velocity, or smoothly hand off to live controls. Never snap back to the original origin at cancellation.
- Use a monotonic clock and elapsed `dt` from the render frame, with time-based (frame-rate-independent) easing/damping. For frame-independent exponential decay use `alpha=1-exp(-lambda * dtSeconds)`; apply proper quaternion shortest-path spherical interpolation to orientations; never blindly lerp Euler angles across ±180°. Use bounded acceleration/jerk or a tuned critically damped spring for handoffs; don't stack smoothing twice or overshoot.
- Maintain explicit transition states: IDLE, PREPARING, PLAYING, PAUSED, CANCELLING, RECOVERING, REDUCED_MOTION. Typed transitions and one pending request policy: deduplicate double taps; last-intent-wins when safe; protect important user-confirmed state. Cleanly abort old prefetch and animation handles. Cancel/restart does not leak objects/events/tweens/RAF loops. Never gate user input on an arbitrary fixed timeout.
- Use real scene-space anchors (doorways, center of room, unit framing positions). No passing camera through walls, ceilings, window panes or furniture. Resolve physical routes via short spline/polyline through verified walkable navigation graph, with collision checks for camera and look target. If safe spatial path not possible, a graceful semantic cut/fade is preferable to clipping.
- Use at most one orchestration system for the SAME element; do not install a zoo of animation libraries. Prefer native CSS for simple composited UI, one tested timeline engine for choreographed multi-element UI, and render-loop delta-time for 3D camera. Bundle impact and licensing must be checked.

## The complete visual grammar — premium, peaceful, never sluggish
1. **Start / landing → Explore:** page is legible immediately; optional 2–3s tasteful title reveal may be skipped with one tap; 3D appears when ready without hard full-page white/black flicker. No forced intro on returning visitor unless deliberately requested.
2. **Building rotation:** low gain and gentle inertia; dragging begins visibly without noticeable dead zone, decelerates consistently, easy to stop; no orbit tilting upside down or camera crossing balconies, horizon remains comfortable. Auto-rotation OFF by default except deliberate cinematic guided tour.
3. **Tower → floor:** smoothly rotate to the chosen face if needed, visually isolate/lift target floor a modest amount, ease the camera to an intelligible medium shot, and keep selected-floor highlight stable. Motion direction must communicate where the floor came from; no random dissolve that hides spatial relationship.
4. **Floor → unit:** maintain framing continuity; ease the floor cutaway into an understandable dollhouse while moving camera toward selected unit. Preserve apartment and floor IDs exactly.
5. **Unit → room:** a deliberately paced, collision-safe dolly or smooth move with natural look-at lead, optionally a subtle short masked blend only when needed to avoid geometry occlusion. Arrive stable, no micro-oscillation or camera shake.
6. **Room → room:** use doorway/walkable path for nearby rooms, directional continuity and small arc-length-dependent duration; long travel should offer shortcut/skip, no exhausting forced flythrough.
7. **Switch 4 Tour camera modes:** align camera target, pose, FOV and velocities; stop old listeners and enter new control system on the same perceived scene. Never teleport between unrelated angles without a declared crossfade. Back and browser history preserve state.
8. **Amenities → back:** for long-distance jumps (apartment→F10 pool→F11 café), use graceful short transition with spatial anchor or discreet fade/cut, not absurd through-building camera travel.
9. **Material/color/finish:** prewarm/prefetch PBR textures and shaders; update canonical selected material atomically; allow a subtle 160–320ms reveal/crossfade if actually efficient. Avoid duplicate meshes/shaders and avoid a color that briefly disagrees with AI/property selection. On low quality, instant accurate material update beats expensive crossfade.
10. **Day→night/seasons/weather:** physically plausible smooth blend for exposure, sky/ambient, sun and shadows; weather visual state may be phased-in, not a heavy instant particle avalanche. Honor concept-vs-real daylight disclaimer.
11. **UI overlays / dropdown / AI sheet:** calm 180–320ms scale+opacity/translate with low visual travel, carefully timed text readability, natural dismissal, no layout shifts, no stacked blur-heavy backdrops. Mobile bottom sheets follow gesture without lag and settle naturally; preserve focus and escape/back action. Stagger only 20–40ms where useful, never 10 repeated cascades.
12. **Cinematic Day in Your Future Home / Presentation:** gently composed shot timing with continuity, limited meaningful camera journeys, natural pause, manual Skip/Exit, immediate control return. If visual smoothness drops, simplify effects before dropping user controls or facts.

## Timing & interpolation — tune, don't hard-code indiscriminately
The following are *initial tuning bands to validate with real users*, not fixed durations for all distances:
- feedback on tap/hover/focus: 60–140 ms perceived acknowledgment (the action must not wait for full transition)
- icon/button microinteraction: 110–180 ms
- tooltip/sheet/modal: 180–320 ms, with attention to mobile keyboard/focus
- section transition: ~360–650 ms for nearby narrative states; keep a way to skip/interrupt
- floor/unit camera framing: ~650–1100 ms, distance and device dependent
- nearby room guided move: ~650–1400 ms, based on verified path distance and rotation
- remote facility jump: ~300–600 ms with semantic bridge, not 3-second flight
- material reveal: ~160–320 ms; fallback immediate when expensive
- environment lighting/sky blend: ~600–1200 ms when not physically misleading
- intro/tour cinematic segments may be longer ONLY when opt-in and skippable.
Prefer critically damped/smoothstep-like or intentional cubic/quintic ease-in-out for composed moves; never apply abrupt linear speed or uniform movement to every object. Avoid excessive overshoot, bounce, wobble, elastic motion, continuous floating, parallax and fake handheld shake. Follow actual user intent: fast click gets immediate acknowledgment, transition feels gentle but is never an obstacle.

## Performance is part of the animation design — measured, not asserted
- 60Hz target on capable desktop corresponds to ~16.7ms/frame; 30Hz low-device viable fallback ~33.3ms/frame, not a promise of universal 60 FPS. For 120Hz device refresh, avoid artificially locking the whole app to 60 if measurement supports smoother rendering. Measure p50/p95/p99 frame time, missed-vsync/jank fraction, long JS tasks, FPS stability, GPU/CPU memory after 20+ scene swaps, page interaction latency and battery/thermal on physical phones. Do not fake screenshots/metrics.
- Proposed *conditional* release objective: capable desktop balanced profile: p95 frame duration <= 20ms while sampled transition is active with no repeated >50ms hitch; midrange mobile balanced/low: p95 <= 35ms and immediate Lite downgrade when sustained p95 worsens. Record frame-hitch count and profile conditions. These are project goals pending actual benchmarking and calibration, **not guarantees**. If devices fail, simplify real-time shaders, reduce dynamic resolution, shadow count, particles, textures and scene concurrency before impairing controls or substituting promotional video for live 3D.
- Ensure all textures/GLBs required by the destination are preloaded/decoded according to memory/network budget; show a stable preview/placeholder and never freeze the camera awaiting a network response. Compile shaders strategically; avoid first-frame stutter after selecting new material; chunk costly work off the input path and avoid blocking reflow. Respect storage and network data-saver settings. Reclaim graphics buffers and controls after changes.
- For HTML animations, default to `transform` and `opacity` instead of animated top/left/height/width or expensive `filter`/`backdrop-filter`. Use `will-change` *only for short-lived measured cases*, remove after the animation, and never place it globally on dozens of panels. Align scroll and 3D motion without multiple RAF schedulers.
- Demand real results on iOS Safari, Android Chrome, desktop Windows Chrome/Edge/Firefox and tablet Safari as available; emulators explicitly labeled; poor devices retain functional, visually calm Lite mode. A 60 FPS average with 400ms stalls is not smooth: track frame-time variance/hitches.
- Clamp the effect of unusually large frame delta after tab resume; do not fast-forward a camera trip into a wall or create huge spring velocities. Pause inactive guided animation and resume consistently; background mode should not consume GPU excessively.

## Comfort / accessibility / input discipline
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is honored at first paint and when preference changes; reduce removes major spatial movement, automated camera fly-through, parallax and large perspective changes. Provide user-visible content and immediate non-spatial transitions; functionality and state remain intact.
- Offer a quiet contextual option **Motion: Gentle (default) / Reduced**; on OS reduced-motion default to Reduced. Option does not become a fifth top-level section. Never infer motion tolerance from disability or force a high-motion mode.
- A visible Skip/Stop/Pause/Back is available during *opt-in tours*; tapping/selecting something new interrupts correctly. Do not lock wheel, touch or keyboard indefinitely. Focus and screen-reader announcements follow stable destination semantics; no repeated announcements each animation frame. All four languages preserve layout and motion intent.
- On touch, a slight finger movement must not trigger unit selection while orbiting. Use drag/tap thresholds, pointer capture, pinch/rotation gesture arbitration and prevention of accidental double transitions. Keep tap-to-move and drag sensitivity adjustable by measured usability.
- Sounds and vibration OFF by default; no surprise music or mechanical whooshes; ambient audio only after user opt-in. All transitions must communicate without audio.

## Motion storyboard and engineering proof
Required deliverables by phase:
A. Pre-build: motion storyboard of 12 essential pathways with source/destination anchors, camera trajectory, duration envelope, focus intent, interruption semantics, lower-tier variant, reduced-motion variant, fallbacks.
B. First *working vertical slice*: exterior rotate -> select floor -> enter unit -> move living room -> swap material -> back to tower; full chain in actual browser with measured frame time and no glitches. Do this BEFORE broad site/content expansion.
C. Instrumentation: in-app DEV-ONLY motion HUD with FPS, frame-time sparkline, p95/p99 and input delay; sample per transition and device tier without leaking telemetry to public users. Export benchmark CSV/JSON and reproducible screen recordings.
D. Motion automated tests: test double-click/rapid retarget, immediate skip, cursor move while animation, low-memory, 4 locales, orientation, OS reduced motion change mid-transition, tab background/resume, WebGL context loss, offline, cold shader compile, repeated 20x navigation cycles, focus and historical Back. Include visual comparison at start, 25%, 50%, 75%, end; measure **motion continuity** beyond static golden screenshots.
E. Usability review: at least 3 representative first-time testers when available, including user watching both desktop and phone. Mark as unperformed if not actually recruited. Ask: 'did camera feel natural', 'could you interrupt instantly', 'was floor relation clear', 'was anything dizzying', 'did it feel responsive'. Collect evidence before declaring P0 pass.
F. Release decision: G8 motion smoothness gate is PASS only when representative real device data, interaction and reduced-motion checks satisfy acceptance and any failures are documented. V7 G1–G7 remain intact. Project remains NOT READY if any P0 is NOT_RUN/BLOCKED.

## Anti-patterns prohibited
Do NOT create artificial smoothness by adding a uniform 2-second ease on every button. No motion blur smearing text, fake slow motion, uninterruptible sequence, blanket crossfade between unrelated 3D states, 20 heavy postprocess passes, 10 layered CSS animations, camera bob, heavy mobile backdrop blur, exponential easing tied to frame count, unbounded long interpolation, camera overshoot into walls, transition-time memory leak, layout thrashing, random jitter, stalling until assets load, or pre-rendered Higgsfield videos presented as interactive 3D.
